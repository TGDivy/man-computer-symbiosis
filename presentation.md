# MAN–COMPUTER SYMBIOSIS

## A live found-film presentation after J. C. R. Licklider, 1960

This is the creative and delivery contract for the implemented 32-scene deck. It is grounded in the complete paper:

<https://groups.csail.mit.edu/medg/people/psz/Licklider.html>

## Core proposition

Licklider's two aims organize the film:

1. Bring computers into formulative technical thinking, while the important question is still emerging.
2. Bring computers into real-time thinking, before a changing situation has moved on.

The working relation is iterative. People set goals, formulate hypotheses, define criteria, and evaluate. Computers perform the routinizable retrieval, transformation, simulation, and comparison that prepares insight and decision. Evidence, anomalies, and alternatives return to the person and make a better next question possible.

The deck does not argue that familiar infrastructure automatically creates symbiosis. It asks what kind of relationship that infrastructure enables—and ends with Licklider's optimistic possibility that the long interim of people and computers thinking together may be “the most creative and exciting” period in human history.

## Presentation form

- The default **Wonder Cut** opens on a ripe green fig tree, then a real fig wasp in its habitat. `?opening=mystery` preserves the darker cut-fig alternate.
- Scene 02 restores the technical specimen-card choreography and identifies the displayed specimen as *Blastophaga psenes* while keeping the habitat species distinction explicit in the notes.
- Scene 03 defines the biological relation, then cuts the entire frame to a black question: “What would symbiosis between a human and a computer require?” Both members of the new pairing are explicit without a literal label-swap animation.
- Scene 00 includes a bottom-left replay control for the leader and its procedural audio.
- The film is intentionally a wide-screen experience. Narrow, short, or portrait viewports receive a period-styled notice asking for a desktop or other wide device.
- Scene 24 is a real browser interaction: microphone recognition when available, visible transcript, speech-synthesis playback, and an honest text fallback.
- Scenes 18 and 27 are temporary room prompts, not data collection. Nothing is persisted, copied, downloaded, transmitted, or tabulated.
- The ending uses this presentation's own production loop as a contemporary example before returning to Licklider's interim and the living fig–wasp relation.

## Sound structure

The projector motor and film hiss remain the physical base. Above them, a procedural score changes at scene and build level rather than looping six static beds. Stereo-spread voices, a restrained low pulse, reverberant motifs, and physical splice/transport cues create pressure, release, and continuity:

| Chapter | Sound role |
|---|---|
| Biological opening | An organic interval gathers beneath the photographs, then drops rapidly to near-silence for Scene 03's black question. |
| Preparing to think | Clerical harmony and a low pulse accumulate through the preparatory operations and peak during the deliberately slow conversion; the clean graph releases that pressure. |
| SYMBIOTE | A restrained period product-film motif gains rhythm as cards and alternative plots move, then breaks at the reel change. |
| Prerequisites | Five tonal voices accumulate one per prerequisite scene, making the infrastructure feel assembled rather than listed; Scene 25 dissolves the pulse beneath the relationship question. |
| The relation | Analytical, organic, and product motifs begin to interlock as the making-of exchange becomes harder to divide neatly. |
| Interim and return | The pulse disappears beneath Licklider's final quotation. Organic, relationship, and product material resolves through the photographic coda, then fades across the archive credits. |

Sound remains local, procedural, muted with **M**, and only starts after a browser gesture.

## Guardrails

- No chatbot, AI logo, generic data-centre hero image, cloud diagram, or server-rack illustration appears.
- Do not make a categorical on-screen fig-species claim. The habitat image is *Pleistodontes*; the scientific specimen is *Blastophaga psenes*; neither is the parasitoid *Apocrypta*.
- The opening and Scene 30 use attributed biological photography. The coda is an evocative three-frame montage inside a fixed film gate, not sequential footage of a single pollination event and not a full-screen digital zoom.
- The historical computer, terminal, library, and control-room assets are identified in `SOURCES.md`.
- Visible datasets, calculations, plots, SYMBIOTE branding, and transfer cards are transparent pedagogical reconstructions.
- “Golden age of human creativity” is spoken interpretation. The exact §1.2 wording shown on screen is “the most creative and exciting in the history of mankind.”
- The current project's making is presented as testimony and a case study, not proof that every human–computer interaction is symbiotic.
