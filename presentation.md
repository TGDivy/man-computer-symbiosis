# MAN–COMPUTER SYMBIOSIS

## A live found-film presentation after J. C. R. Licklider, 1960

This is the current creative and delivery contract for the implemented 35-scene deck. It is based on the complete text of Licklider's paper:

<https://groups.csail.mit.edu/medg/people/psz/Licklider.html>

## Core proposition

Licklider's two aims lead the presentation:

1. Bring computers into the formulative parts of technical thinking, where the important question is still emerging.
2. Bring computers into real-time thinking, where a situation cannot wait for a fully predetermined procedure.

The closing statement is explicit:

> People set goals, hypotheses, criteria, and evaluations. Computers do the routinizable work that prepares insight and decision.

The deck does not argue that any familiar technology automatically creates this partnership. Its question is how people and computing systems work together.

## Presentation form

- The opening defaults to the brighter **Wonder Cut**: a ripe fig on its tree, then a real fig wasp within a fig.
- Add **?opening=mystery** to preserve the prior cut-fig/specimen emphasis as a live alternate.
- Scene 00 has a replay control that restarts the leader, animation, and local procedural audio without a reload.
- Scene 26 is a real browser interaction: speech recognition when supplied by the browser, a live transcript, speech-synthesis playback, and a text fallback when recognition is unavailable.
- Audience records in Scenes 18 and 29 stay in the browser. They support Copy, CSV download, and print/screenshot-friendly display without a backend.

## Live controls

- **Space**, **Enter**, **ArrowRight**, click, or tap: advance one build or scene
- **ArrowLeft**: return one build or scene
- **M**: mute local procedural sound
- **N**: speaker notes
- **R**: references and attributions
- **F**: fullscreen
- **Escape**: close a panel
- **Home** / **End**: leader / credits

## Guardrails

- No chatbot, AI logo, generic data-centre hero imagery, or server-rack illustration appears in the deck.
- The fig wasp is shown as a real Pleistodontes species in a fig. The presentation does not put an unreliable categorical species claim on screen.
- The opening and closing use real biological photography. No synthetic wasp animation is used.
- The historical computer, terminal, library, and command-room assets are identified in SOURCES.md.
- The visible datasets and alternate plot outputs are pedagogical reconstructions, not Licklider's historical data.
- The time-sharing parallel to contemporary pooled access belongs in the spoken bridge, rather than being reduced to a cloud-infrastructure image.
