# Source and Asset Register

This register separates primary text, paraphrase, interpretation, illustrative reconstruction, and archival imagery used in the presentation.

## Primary Paper

J. C. R. Licklider, “Man-Computer Symbiosis,” *IRE Transactions on Human Factors in Electronics*, volume HFE-1, pages 4–11, March 1960.

- Full text hosted by MIT: <https://groups.csail.mit.edu/medg/people/psz/Licklider.html>
- The presentation was developed against the complete paper, not a summary.
- Section numbers below follow the MIT-hosted transcription.

## Explicit Interpretations

These lines are presentation arguments, not quotations from Licklider. Each is visibly labelled **OUR INTERPRETATION** in the deck.

- “Automating the plotting is not automating the thinking.”
- “Automate the distance between interesting thoughts.”
- “Not a pipeline. A conversation with representations.”
- “The computer is not the answer. It might help you discover the question.”
- “Where is the friction between one interesting thought and the next?”
- “What do you do in order to think that you frequently mistake for thinking?”
- “Compress the distance between interesting thoughts.”

## Illustrative Reconstructions

- **Scenes 09–10:** The six datasets and finished speech-intelligibility plot are invented to stage the operation Licklider describes. They are not presented as his historical data.
- **Scene 12:** SYMBIOTE™ is a fictional 1960 research product. Its prompts paraphrase capabilities described in §4.
- **Scene 16:** The drawable desk-surface display is an interactive reconstruction inspired by §5.5.1, not a historical interface.
- **Scene 17:** The microphone test is theatrical and does not request microphone access.
- **Scene 18:** The NORAD image illustrates a period shared-display environment; it is not Licklider’s proposed thinking centre.
